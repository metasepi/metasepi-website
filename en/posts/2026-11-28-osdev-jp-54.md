[Takibi](https://github.com/takibi-lang/takibi) now runs Alpine Linux's BusyBox HTTPd: an unmodified AArch64 binary dynamically linked with musl. In the demo, Takibi boots in QEMU and serves files from an ext2 root filesystem.

I follow a single HTTP request through the kernel, from receiving a TCP connection and creating a worker with Copy-on-Write (COW) to reading a file, sending the response, and reaping the worker.

My main interest is **what the compiler can check along the way**:

- Explicit byte-order conversion when parsing network packets.
- Proof that a packet slice stays within the receive buffer.
- Matching process record evidence to the correct address-space root.
- Mandatory handling of the TCP send result.

These are concrete examples of how building a language and a kernel together can turn potential runtime defects into compile-time errors. Other problems, such as statically checking the lifetime of COW source pages, remain open.
